# 📦 About the Japanese second-hand market

## Introduction

Most of the components required for this conversion, *apart from the small supplies*, will not be found on public sale. This is precise, specialised arcade hardware, sold only by the official manufacturers. It is obviously completely impossible to go through Sega's after-sales service, so we can only turn to person-to-person sales.

As a result, **we are dependent on the availability of items on these second-hand markets**. While it is not very hard, for example, to find a Sega IO4 board or an Aime reader, it is much harder to find an ALLS HX2 and practically impossible to find an official touchscreen from a maimai DX cabinet. For some of these parts, alternatives exist; for others, there will be no choice but to be patient and stay on the lookout for an offer on the Japanese second-hand market.

!!! tip "Where do the spare parts come from?"
    Most of the spare parts on the Japanese second-hand market come from cabinets that have been dismantled to be liquidated more easily. Most often following the closure of the game center that owned it, or because the cabinet in question no longer draws a crowd. In the case of maimai DX, since the game is very popular, it is rare for a cabinet to be sold for export, and rarer still for one of them to be taken apart to be sold as spare parts.

    Fortunately, most of the components of Sega cabinets are interchangeable and reused from one cabinet to another, including in machines far less popular than maimai DX, for which it is infinitely more likely to find spare parts.

## Buying via a proxy

Most of the parts listed in the [shopping list](equipment.md) are found on JDirectItems Auction, the largest auction site in Japan. The problem is that this site (like the vast majority of Japanese sales sites) requires a Japanese address, phone number and payment method in order to bid and buy. Without residing in Japan, it is therefore impossible to access it directly.

This is where **proxy buying services** come in. These are companies that have an address and an account in Japan, and that will bid, buy and collect the packages on your behalf, before forwarding them to you internationally. They take a commission plus service fees for this work, on top of the price of the item and the shipping fees.

There are several services of this kind, among them:

* **[Neokyo]({{NEOKYO}})**
* **[Zenmarket]({{ZENMARKET}})**
* **[FROM JAPAN]({{FROM_JAPAN}})**
* **[Buyee]({{BUYEE}})**: one of the best known and oldest, but see the warning below.

!!! warning "Ban on shipping electronic parts on Buyee"
    Buyee refuses to ship internationally any item containing a lithium cell or battery, in application of air regulations on the transport of dangerous goods. However, some of the parts we are looking for (for example the ALLS HX2) carry a small lithium coin cell to power their internal clock (RTC) even when powered off. In practice, this therefore amounts to a shipping ban.

    For these items, prefer a service like Neokyo or Zenmarket, which do not apply this same restriction.

The general way it works stays similar from one service to another:

* You create an account.
* You submit the link of the item you are interested in (or you browse JDirectItems Auction directly from their site).
* You credit your account with the necessary amount, then they handle the purchase.
* The proxy receives your item and stores it for you.
    * Most proxy services store the item for free for a certain period.
    * You can thus accumulate several items in their warehouse in order to ship them all in a single package.
    * Once ready, make a packing request: the proxy team will pack your items and ship them to your address outside Japan.

Once the item is received in their Japanese warehouse, you can generally request additional photos before validating the final shipment, which is particularly useful for checking the condition of a second-hand part before it travels halfway around the globe. It will not refund you if there is a problem, but you will at least know whether to re-order the part elsewhere, without waiting for the package to reach you physically.

## Tips for searching

Finding the right part on JDirectItems Auction can be frustrating if you do not know a few basics:

* **Search in Japanese.** A search in English or French will almost never return relevant results. Instead, use the part and reference names as they appear on the official diagrams and manuals (for example `ALLS HX2`, `IO4`, `Aime`), which generally stay the same in Japanese since they are commercial references. Feel free to go through a translator (DeepL, Google Translate) to understand the title and the full description of a listing.
* **Spot the word "ジャンク" (*junk*).** Working hardware is often sold with this label, simply because it has not been tested recently. It is therefore not necessarily broken, it is just sold "as is", with no warranty.
* **Check the seller.** The percentage of positive ratings (ratings at the top right of the listing) is a good indicator of reliability. A professional seller used to dismantling arcade cabinets is generally a mark of seriousness.
* **Look carefully at the photos.** Japanese sellers tend to be very thorough with photos, even without an English description. Zoom in on the connectors, the condition of the pins and any labels or markings that let you confirm the exact part number.
* **Keep an eye on the auction end time.** JDirectItems auctions end at Japanese time (JST, UTC+9), often in the middle of the night in Europe. Most proxy services let you place a maximum automatic bid in advance, which avoids having to stay awake to outbid at the last second.
* **Anticipate the extra fees.** The displayed price is never the final price: add the proxy service's commission, the shipping fees to their warehouse, then the international shipping fees (often calculated by weight **and** volume). A compact electronic board generally stays reasonable to ship, but keep this calculation in mind before bidding too high on a rare part. Also anticipate any customs fees on receipt, which vary depending on your country of residence.
* **Set up an alert.** Most proxy services let you save a search and receive a notification as soon as a new matching item is listed. This is particularly useful for rare parts like the ALLS HX2, which can take weeks, or even months, to reappear.
